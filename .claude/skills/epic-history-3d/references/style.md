# The look: three references, one style

The user asked for three directions. Take the **general idea** of each and never copy a channel's maps, intro, logo, branding colours, music or compositions.

## 1. Map style: territory maps that grow and change (the History Mapped Out direction)
- **Clean, readable political maps.** Each state is one flat, saturated-but-soft colour at about 60% opacity, with a darker border line and a soft inner glow. Coasts are crisp with an ink line and a light halo.
- **Change over time is the story.** Territories spread outward from the capital when conquered, recede when lost, and flash softly the moment they change hands. A date cartouche counts the years as the borders move.
- **Few labels, big and clear.** One label per state in spaced capitals, sea names in pale italic tone, and a handful of key cities with name plates. Remove labels that are no longer relevant (`t_out`).
- **Calm camera on maps.** Mostly slow pushes, drifts and pull-backs that reveal the bigger picture, with a faster fly-to when the story jumps to a new place.

## 2. Storytelling: the campaign-driven narrative (the Epic History direction)
- **Map-first narration.** The map is the stage, and armies, arrows and battles happen on it.
- **Thick campaign arrows** in the faction colour, outlined, drawn on as the narrator says "advanced", "invaded" or "marched".
- **Army figurines** that move across the map, meet, clash (crossed swords and smoke), and topple when defeated.
- **Battle diagrams** become full 3D battlefields: formations in ranks, flanking arrows on the ground, charges, a unit routing.
- **Chapters** open with a title card (carved stone), and people are introduced with a portrait card when the user supplies an image.
- **Rhythm:** maps for context, then a location or battle for the drama, then back to the map. Every 3-9 s something new happens on screen, synced to the word.

## 3. Visuals: a historical strategy game
- **A 3D relief campaign map.** Real coastlines, rivers and lakes (Natural Earth), true mountain ranges with hillshade and snow on high peaks, and clouds drifting over the map that the camera can dive through.
- **Toy-like strategy pieces.** Toon-shaded models with ink outlines (castles, towns, tents, ships, soldiers in instanced ranks), banners waving in the wind, and gilded bases under the army figurines.
- **Game UI touches inside the world.** A parchment date cartouche, parchment name plates hovering over cities and armies, and a carved stone for titles.
- **Light and atmosphere.** A painted sky dome, a sun with real shadows, fog for depth, dust, embers and birds, plus a film finish (vignette, grain, gentle grade).
- **Mood through light.** Dawn for beginnings, day for campaigns, dusk for decisive battles, night or storm for loss.

## Colour
- One colour per faction for the whole film, chosen to be distinct (greens, reds, purples, blues, ochres). Avoid two similar hues on neighbouring states.
- Arrows are a brighter or contrasting shade so they read on top of the territory.
- Use the `parchment` palette for ancient or early-medieval stories, `campaign` (the default) for most, and `dusk` for grim chapters.
